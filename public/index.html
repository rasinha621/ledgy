Categories

Each month has three budget categories. Each category is a card on the month page.

Wants
Things that you want but do not need. Examples are restaurants, hobbies and trips.
Needs
Costs that you must pay. Examples are rent, utilities, insurance and food.
Savings
Money that you keep for the future. The default rows are 401k, HSA, Roth, Cash and Investing.
Groceries and Transit
These are lists. A list adds up the items that you type in it. A row in Needs can show the total of a list.
Your own lists
Select Add new list to make a list. Put the list in Wants, Needs or Savings.

Each row has a menu at the right side. Select the arrow (▾) to open or close the menu. The arrow turns up when the menu is open. Use the menu to do these tasks:

  • Link a Wants or Needs row to a savings goal.
  • Delete the row.

The top of the month page shows these values:

Net income
The money that you receive after tax. Select it to open the Income window.
Allocated
The sum of Wants, Needs and Savings.
Amount remaining
Net income minus Allocated. A negative value means that you spend more than you earn.

50/30/20 budget

The 50/30/20 rule divides your net income into three parts:

  • 50% for Needs.
  • 30% for Wants.
  • 20% for Savings.

To use this rule in Ledgy, do these steps:

  1. Select Settings.
  2. In Budget breakdown, set Needs to 50, Wants to 30 and Savings to 20. These are the default values.
  3. Select Income and enter your pay for each month.
  4. Enter your costs in each category.

Each card shows its budget and the percent that you have used. Aim to keep each percent at 100% or less.

Contribution limits

Each year, the IRS sets a maximum amount that you can put in each account. The limits change almost every year. Ledgy does not know the limits. Find them and enter them yourself.

401k
Search for "IRS 401(k) limits". The IRS publishes the new limit in a news release in the fall.
HSA
Search for "IRS HSA limits". The limit is in the yearly IRS Revenue Procedure. The limit is different for self-only and family coverage. People age 55 or older can add an extra amount.
Roth
Search for "IRS IRA contribution limits". The Roth IRA limit is the same as the IRA limit. A high income can decrease your limit. Depending on your income, you may not be able to contribute to a Roth IRA.

To enter a limit, select Settings. Type the value in Annual contribution limits. The Summary page then shows your progress for each year. If you type 0, Ledgy shows no limit.

Savings goals

A savings goal is money that you keep for one purpose, for example a trip. Each goal adds one row to Savings in each month. Type the money that you save in that row.

To make a goal, do these steps:

  1. Select Settings.
  2. Select Add savings goal.
  3. Type a name and a target amount.
  4. Select Save.

When you spend money from a goal, link the spend to the goal. To link a spend, do these steps:

  1. Find the row in Wants or Needs.
  2. Select the arrow (▾) at the right side of the row.
  3. Below Paid from a goal, select the goal. The menu shows the money that is left in each goal.

A linked row has a colored line on the left side of its amount. Put the pointer on the note to see the name of the goal. To remove the link, open the menu again and select Not from a goal.

A linked spend has these results:

  • The spend stays in Wants or Needs. It is part of the month total.
  • Ledgy subtracts the spend from the goal. The goal shows the money that you saved minus the money that you spent.

To see your goals on the Summary page, select Customize. In Summary: savings columns, select each goal. The value is the money that is left in the goal. Put the pointer on the value to see the money that you saved and spent. The value is green when the money that you saved is equal to or more than the target.

If you remove a goal, Ledgy removes its Savings rows. The spends that you linked to the goal stay, but they have no link.

Note: The Excel export does not keep the links between spends and goals.
