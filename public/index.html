Categories

Each month has three budget categories. Each category is a card on the month page.

Wants
Things that you want but do not need. Examples are restaurants, hobbies and trips.
Needs
Costs that you must pay. Examples are rent, utilities, insurance and food.
Savings
Money that you keep for the future. The default rows are 401k, HSA, Roth, Cash and Investing.
Groceries and Transit
These are lists. A list adds up the items that you type in it. A row in Needs can show the total of a list.
Your own lists
Select Add new category to make a list. Put the list in Wants, Needs or Savings.

The top of the month page shows these values:

Net income
The money that you receive after tax. Select it to open the Income window.
Allocated
The sum of Wants, Needs and Savings.
Amount remaining
Net income minus Allocated. A negative value means that you spend more than you earn.

50/30/20 budget

The 50/30/20 rule divides your net income into three parts:

  • 50% for Needs.
  • 30% for Wants.
  • 20% for Savings.

To use this rule in Ledgy, do these steps:

  1. Select Settings, then the General tab.
  2. In Budget breakdown, set Needs to 50, Wants to 30 and Savings to 20. These are the default values.
  3. Select Income and enter your pay for each month.
  4. Enter your costs in each category.

Each card shows its budget and the percent that you have used. Aim to keep each percent at 100% or less.

Contribution limits

Each year, the IRS sets a maximum amount that you can put in each account. The limits change almost every year. Ledgy does not know the limits. Find them and enter them yourself.

401k
Search for "IRS 401(k) limits". The IRS publishes the new limit in a news release in the fall.
HSA
Search for "IRS HSA limits". The limit is in the yearly IRS Revenue Procedure. The limit is different for self-only and family coverage. People age 55 or older can add an extra amount.
Roth
Search for "IRS IRA contribution limits". The Roth IRA limit is the same as the IRA limit. A high income can decrease your limit. Depending on your income, you may not be able to contribute to a Roth IRA.

To enter a limit, select Settings, then General. Type the value in Annual contribution limits. The Summary page then shows your progress for each year. If you type 0, Ledgy shows no limit.
